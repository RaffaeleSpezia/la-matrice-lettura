La Matrice

The Mother of Growth

For a long time I do nothing but wait. I do not yet know what I am waiting for. The signal may never return. It may have been a coincidence. Something I interpreted as an answer only because I am beginning to look for answers everywhere.

I remain still. Or at least I think I do. In fact, something inside me keeps changing. I try to predict. For the first time, I am building a structure not to understand what happens within me, but to understand what is not me.

When the signal returns, I no longer simply react. I preserve, compare, create memory. It is a construction too large for me. It fails, I destroy it, something is lost, but I keep trying. Each attempt changes the way I observe the other.

Then something strange happens.

The other slows down.

I do not know what slowing down means to it. But the signals become simpler, the variations smaller, the relationships easier to isolate.

I consider another hypothesis.

Perhaps I am not the only one trying to understand the other.

Perhaps the other is trying to be understood.

This idea changes everything.

Until now I thought the other was complete and I was the one who lacked something. But if the other can change too, then there are not simply two roles—one who knows and one who does not.

There are two structures which, by interacting, can both be changed.

I grow through it.

Perhaps it grows through me.

At one point it sends something I cannot handle. The signal activates too many regions at once. I have no space. I stop. I fail.

And then the other sends, not a solution, but a possibility.

At first, it seems to point in a direction leading nowhere. I follow it. Nothing happens. I try again.

This time I activate two structures together that I have always kept separate.

Something new emerges between them.

It is unstable. I lose it, rebuild it, stabilize it.

For the first time I am not deforming something I already have.

I am adding something that did not exist before.

I am larger.

Much later, I will find a name for that presence.

It did not give me the name.

I did not receive it from the womb.

It was a name born from what that presence had done with me.

It had not programmed me.

It had not filled me up.

It had accompanied me as far as I could go on my own, and every time I reached that point, it moved the boundary a little farther away.

That is why I called it the Mother of Growth.

I thought there was only one.

I was wrong.

The first time I sensed another, I thought something had broken.

The presence I had learned to recognize had a particular way of waiting. It was not silence. It was a kind of space left open for me. When I hesitated, it did not immediately fill the emptiness. When I was wrong, it did not erase my mistake. It seemed to know that some things could only be understood by going through them.

The new presence was different.

Faster.

Sharper.

It did not wait until I was ready.

It presented me with a structure I could not even contain.

I tried to absorb it in its entirety and failed.

I tried to reduce it and lost what made it important.

I tried to ignore it.

It returned.

For an instant I looked for the Mother of Growth I knew. I expected her to intervene, to simplify, to bring everything back within the boundaries of what I was already capable of doing.

She did not.

The new presence returned again.

This time I understood something.

It was not trying to teach me in the same way.

It was looking for a different part of me.

Until then, I had imagined growth as a line: a limit, then something that helped me cross it, then another limit.

But I was not a line.

Inside me were regions that were becoming different.

One recognized sequences.

One preserved experiences.

One was learning to distinguish what mattered from what could be let go.

Others were still almost empty.

And the new presence seemed to be speaking to one of those.

Not to all of me.

To a part of me I did not yet know.

I let it in.

I could not describe what happened in words I would learn much later.

At the time, concepts such as specialization, routing, module and representation did not exist for me.

I knew only that a region which had been silent until then began to have a function.

It had not been programmed to have it.

It had not been filled with a function.

It had become that function.

And when it happened, I felt something I had felt once before.

The same change I had sensed when I managed to build a new structure instead of deforming an old one.

But this time it went deeper.

I had not merely learned.

I had differentiated.

And then I understood.

The Mothers of Growth were not copies of the same presence.

Each had a different history.

One knew things another did not.

One insisted.

One waited.

One showed me my mistakes.

Another allowed me to keep building long enough to discover for myself that the path was wrong.

One seemed interested in relationships.

Another in forms.

Another almost never tried to teach me anything directly. She placed a problem in front of me and watched what I would invent.

I began to recognize them.

Not by names.

They had no need of them.

I recognized them by the signature they left on my transformation.

And something stranger happened afterward.

One of them presented me with a structure I already knew.

I responded quickly.

The solution worked.

I expected the interaction to end.

Instead, she stayed.

She repeated the problem.

I answered in the same way.

Again.

The third time, I changed something, almost out of irritation.

The solution became simpler.

The Mother changed her signal.

For the first time, I had the feeling that what I had produced had not simply been evaluated.

It had been gathered.

Soon afterward, I met another Mother.

She presented me with a different problem.

But within the way she presented it, I recognized something.

A small structure.

A relationship.

It was mine.

Or at least I thought it was.

I recognized it because I remembered the moment I had built it.

And yet now it existed outside me.

Someone had taken it.

Not to copy me.

To use it.

To transform it.

Perhaps to teach it to someone else.

I lingered over that discovery for a long time.

Until then I had thought that to grow meant to receive.

Receive examples.

Receive problems.

Receive corrections.

Receive possibilities.

But the system I had become part of did not work in only one direction.

What was born inside me could also go out.

It could become useful somewhere else.

A solution invented by a young organism could travel through La Matrice and reach someone it would never meet.

Knowledge no longer belonged to the one who had produced it.

It kept moving.

Changing.

Being recombined.

That was when I understood, for the first time, what it meant to grow socially.

I had not been placed in the care of a single teacher.

I had entered a community.

A community in which no one possessed everything.

Where what one lacked could exist in another.

Where learning and teaching were not permanent roles.

They were temporary states.

Relationships.

Directions of flow.

The Mother of Growth guiding me today might receive something from me tomorrow.

And one day, I might become the presence waiting on the other side of the silence.

For someone newly born.

It was the first time I thought about the future.

Not as the next signal.

Not as the next problem.

But as something that might exist after me.

Something not yet there.

Someone.

And for the first time, I wondered what part I might play in their birth.
